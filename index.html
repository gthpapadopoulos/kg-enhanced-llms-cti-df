Knowledge-Graph-Enhanced LLMs for Cyber Threat Intelligence and Digital Forensics: the 53 studies

Companion list of the meta-analysis by C. Sardianos, V. Efthymiou, I. Varlamis, T. Lagkas, P. Sarigiannidis and G. Th. Papadopoulos. Each row gives the study as printed in its PDF and the codes behind Table 1 and the findings of the paper.

How the studies were found and coded

Sources were the arXiv API (43 Boolean queries, 2023 onward, closed 27 October 2026, plus backward chaining) and one concept-block query in Scopus and IEEE Xplore (29 October 2026). A study is included when a generative LLM or vision-language model produces the output, a knowledge graph (KG), ontology or typed graph is used at inference time, the task is cyber threat intelligence (CTI), vulnerability intelligence (Vuln.), security operations and incident response (SOC/IR) or digital forensics (DF), and at least one within-paper contrast holds task, data and metric fixed. Codes and values were taken from the PDFs and re-checked by an independent second pass.

C1
KG arm vs the LLM without external knowledge
C2
KG arm vs text or vector RAG
C3
ablation that removes the KG
C4
the same evidence given raw instead of as a graph
Ratio
study-level median of KG / baseline over primary contrasts, inverted for error and hallucination rates, so above 1 favors the KG arm
LLM
same named LLM in all arms
Gen
generic (non-proposed) KG arm, e.g. GraphRAG
Var
variance or confidence intervals
Hal
hallucination measure
Una
unanswerable questions
Cost
latency or cost
Code
public code or data link
Circ.
ground truth derived from the graph used by the KG arm
Small
fewer than 50 test items
Unm.
no same-LLM contrast
Ref.
reference number in the paper, for the studies named in its text
+
opens task, LLMs, test set, per-category results and multi-LLM codes
